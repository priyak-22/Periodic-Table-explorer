Hydrogen (H)

Hydrogen is the lightest and simplest chemical element in the periodic table. It has an atomic number of 1, meaning it contains only one proton and one electron. Due to its simple structure, hydrogen is highly reactive and rarely exists in its free form on Earth.

Hydrogen is the most abundant element in the universe and plays a vital role in the life cycle of stars. In stellar cores, hydrogen atoms undergo nuclear fusion to form helium, releasing enormous amounts of energy.

On Earth, hydrogen is commonly found combined with other elements, such as oxygen in water and carbon in organic compounds. It is widely used in fuel cells, petroleum refining, ammonia production, and space rocket propulsion.

Helium (He)

Helium is a colorless, odorless, and non-reactive noble gas with an atomic number of 2. It is the second lightest element in the periodic table and exists as a monoatomic gas under normal conditions.

Helium is formed in large quantities through nuclear fusion in stars and is one of the most abundant elements in the universe. Due to its very low density, helium can escape Earth’s gravity, making it relatively rare on our planet.

On Earth, helium is mainly obtained from natural gas deposits. It is widely used in cryogenics for cooling superconducting magnets, in balloons and airships, in deep-sea diving mixtures, and in leak detection systems due to its inert nature.
