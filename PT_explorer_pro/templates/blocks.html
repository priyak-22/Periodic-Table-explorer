Understanding Periodic Table Blocks

S-Block

Includes Group 1 (Alkali Metals) & Group 2 (Alkaline Earth Metals).
Electron configuration ends in s-orbital.
Highly reactive metals, especially with water and oxygen.
Soft metals (like Lithium, Sodium) and low melting points.
Good conductors of electricity.
Mostly metallic and form basic oxides.
Some elements are radioactive (Actinides) and used in nuclear energy.
Important in biological systems (e.g., Calcium for bones, Sodium & Potassium for nerve function).
Used in high-tech industries, batteries, aerospace, and nuclear reactors.
Examples: Lithium, Sodium, Calcium, Magnesium, Uranium, Cerium.
Trends:
- Reactivity increases down the group.
- Atomic and ionic radii increase down the group.
- Ionization energy decreases down the group.

P-Block

Includes Groups 13 to 18 elements.
Electron configuration ends in p-orbital.
Contains metals, metalloids, and non-metals.
Reactivity varies: metals are moderately reactive, non-metals like halogens are highly reactive.
Forms acidic oxides and covalent compounds.
Many elements are essential for life (e.g., Carbon, Nitrogen, Oxygen, Phosphorus).
Used in semiconductors, fertilizers, explosives, and plastics.
Physical properties vary: some are solids (like Phosphorus), liquids (like Bromine), or gases (like Nitrogen and Oxygen).
Trends:
- Electronegativity increases across the period.
- Atomic size decreases across the period.
- Ionization energy increases across the period.
Examples: Carbon, Nitrogen, Oxygen, Phosphorus, Sulfur, Chlorine, Argon.

D-Block

Includes Groups 3 to 12 elements (Transition Metals).
Electron configuration ends in d-orbital.
All are metals with high melting points and densities.
Exhibit variable oxidation states and form colored compounds.
Good conductors of heat and electricity.
Often used as catalysts in chemical reactions.
Many are important in industry: steel production, electronics, alloys.
Chemically less reactive than S-block metals.
Trends:
- Atomic size decreases across the period.
- Ionization energy increases across the period.
- Metallic character decreases across the period.
Examples: Iron, Copper, Zinc, Silver, Gold, Chromium, Manganese.

F-Block

Contains Lanthanides (Period 6) and Actinides (Period 7).
Electron configuration ends in f-orbital.
All are metals, mostly radioactive (especially Actinides).
Lanthanides are called rare earth metals, used in magnets, lasers, and electronics.
Actinides include Uranium and Plutonium, used in nuclear energy and weapons.
Soft metals with high melting points.
Reactivity is moderate, usually forming +3 oxidation state compounds.
Many are important in high-tech and defense industries.
Trends:
- Atomic and ionic radii decrease slightly across the series (lanthanide contraction).
- Magnetic and optical properties vary widely.
Examples: Cerium, Neodymium, Uranium, Thorium, Plutonium.
